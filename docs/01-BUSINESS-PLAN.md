# JanSan Sweep — Business Plan

## Executive Summary

A productized, founder-delivered B2B service for independent commercial-cleaning companies. It works the client's existing inventory of legitimate commercial proposals that received no clear decision.

The service solves two related problems: owners spend time earning walkthroughs and preparing proposals but do not consistently complete follow-up, and old opportunities remain ambiguous, leaving owners without a reliable next step, loss reason, or future rebid date.

The first offer is a seven-business-day, $199 prepaid sprint covering up to 20 qualified dormant proposals. JanSan Sweep cleans the list, follows up manually using approved language, hands live interest back to the owner, and delivers a final outcome dashboard.

The business should begin as a manual service. No custom software should be built until at least three paid pilots reveal the same repeated bottleneck and demonstrate demand for continuing service.

## Business Definition

**One-sentence description:** JanSan Sweep follows up on dormant commercial-cleaning proposals and turns silence into a documented decision, conversation, revision request, correct contact, or future rebid date.

**Category:** Productized B2B sales-support service; proposal follow-up and pipeline reactivation; vertical service for commercial cleaners; founder-led acquisition and delivery; future software-assisted service, only after validation.

### What JanSan Sweep Is

- Follow-up on real proposals the client already sent
- A structured, approved human-contact campaign
- Pipeline cleanup and classification
- Immediate handoff of interested prospects
- A small test before hiring sales staff or buying more software

### What JanSan Sweep Is Not

- Cold lead generation
- Purchased contact data
- Automated mass outreach
- An AI voice agent
- Debt collection
- Proposal pricing or contract negotiation
- A guarantee of appointments, revenue, or signed contracts
- A replacement for the client's estimator or CRM

## Founder Advantage

JanSan Sweep matches Clay's strongest immediately monetizable skills: cold calling, appointment setting, prospecting, sales-system design, research, and rapid technical implementation.

The technical advantage should remain behind the service initially. Claude and Perplexity can assist with research, scripts, classification, and internal process design. Antigravity and Claude Code can create a simple credibility page and later internal tools, but only after sales activity begins.

The constraint of having no investment capital is compatible with this model because initial delivery can use an existing phone, business email, Google Sheets, Google Drive, and current AI subscriptions.

## Customer Problem

Commercial-cleaning owners frequently handle selling while also managing cleaners, schedules, inspections, supplies, customer problems, and payroll. A serious commercial proposal may require prospecting, a facility walkthrough, scope development, labor calculations, pricing, and revision.

The core hypothesis is that follow-up becomes inconsistent after the first one or two attempts. The proposal remains technically "open," but nobody owns the next action.

Common dormant statuses include: prospect selected another vendor but never replied; decision was postponed; budget moved to another quarter; original contact left; scope or cleaning frequency changed; incumbent retained but a future renewal date exists; decision-maker requires a revised conversation; proposal was never reviewed; or the opportunity is dead and should be closed.

A clear "no" is still useful because it removes false pipeline and may reveal a loss reason or future rebid date.

## Competitive Position

| Alternative | Function | JanSan Sweep Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Owner self-follow-up | No cash expense | Dedicated execution when the owner lacks time |
| Internal salesperson | Full sales capacity | Small pilot without payroll commitment |
| Appointment setter | Generates new meetings | Works opportunities already created |
| CRM automation | Reminders and sequences | Adds cleanup, human calls, outcomes, and handoffs |
| Cleaning bid software | Prices/sends proposals | Starts after the proposal exists |
| Virtual assistant | General flexible labor | Cleaning-specific sprint, scripts, and outcomes |

Do not claim JanSan Sweep has no competition. Its advantage must come from vertical focus, transparent human execution, clean data, disciplined tracking, and continually improving operating knowledge.

## SWOT

### Strengths

- Strong founder skill fit in cold calling and appointment setting
- Near-zero cash requirement
- Easy-to-understand, tightly scoped pilot
- Works existing opportunity inventory rather than requiring ad spend
- Can be delivered manually before development
- Vertical positioning is more credible than generic lead generation

### Weaknesses

- No proof, testimonials, or benchmarks yet
- Founder-dependent delivery
- Client data may be inconsistent
- Third-party follow-up requires careful trust and disclosure
- Low initial price may attract poor-fit clients

### Opportunities

- Ongoing follow-up retainers
- Pipeline audits
- Renewal and rebid-date management
- Internal workflow software informed by real delivery
- Adjacent verticals such as landscaping, security, restoration, and facility maintenance after cleaning is validated

### Threats

- CRM and proposal platforms add easier follow-up automation
- Clients decide to assign internal staff
- Regulation and litigation risk from improper calling practices
- Low-quality competitors overpromise outcomes
- Commercial cleaners may have too little organized proposal inventory

## Growth Roadmap

### Phase 1: Founder Validation

One niche, one sprint, one founder, manual delivery, three clients maximum, no automated outbound contact.

### Phase 2: Productized Service

Standard intake, refined agreement, repeatable dashboard, $299–$750 sprint tests, optional part-time trained human support, monthly follow-up option.

### Phase 3: Managed Bid Desk

New-proposal cadence plus dormant recovery, dedicated client queues, review and renewal dates, client response SLAs, quality assurance and call coaching, recurring revenue.

### Phase 4: Software-Assisted Service

Internal operations console, client visibility dashboard, role-based access, CSV and approved CRM imports, opt-out suppression, reporting automation, human-controlled communications.

### Phase 5: Vertical Expansion

Only after cleaning economics are proven, test adjacent recurring B2B services where companies perform site visits and send proposals (landscaping, security, restoration, facility maintenance). Keep each vertical's script, terminology, compliance issues, and sales cycle separate.
