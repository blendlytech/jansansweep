# JanSan Sweep — Compliance and Data Handling

This document is operational guidance, not legal advice. Qualified counsel should review the service agreement and outreach model before scaling, automating communications, texting, recording calls, or operating broadly across states.

## Calling Guardrails

- Make live, manually dialed B2B calls
- Prefer publicly listed business numbers
- Do not use an AI voice, prerecorded message, ringless voicemail, or autodialer
- Do not cold text mobile numbers
- Use accurate caller ID
- State Clay's name, JanSan Sweep, the client relationship, and purpose truthfully
- Keep internal suppression lists for JanSan Sweep and each campaign
- Honor do-not-call requests immediately
- Do not conceal the cleaning-company client
- Do not claim prior engagement without evidence
- Do not misrepresent results, affiliations, pricing, or urgency
- Do not contact restricted government-procurement officials
- Review state rules before entering new jurisdictions
- Do not record calls without reviewing applicable consent laws

## Email Guardrails

Sprint follow-up emails are commercial email and must carry, in every message:

- Accurate sender name and reply-to address; no disguised headers
- A subject line that reflects the actual content
- A valid physical postal address — use `PO Box 2524, Rimrock, AZ 86335`, never the home address
- A working opt-out instruction, honored immediately and permanently and logged to the suppression list

No mass blasting, no purchased lists, no tracking pixels in outreach email.

## Data Governance

### Minimum Data

Collect only business information necessary to perform the sprint. Do not accept consumer lists, card data, Social Security numbers, medical information, bank information, or unrelated employee/customer records.

### Security

- Use one access-controlled folder per client
- Limit access to Clay and authorized client users
- Disable public link sharing
- Enable two-factor authentication
- Keep client campaign data separate from JanSan Sweep's own sales prospecting data
- Never place account passwords inside a spreadsheet

### AI Tool Usage

Until client authorization and service terms are clear:

- Do not upload full client proposal files or identifiable campaign lists to AI services
- Use redacted examples to develop scripts
- Use AI for templates, process design, and code without identifiable client data
- If AI becomes part of delivery, disclose it, minimize data, review terms, and obtain written authorization

### Retention (Draft — Have Counsel Review)

- Retain working records during the sprint
- Deliver the final report
- Confirm client receipt
- Delete or return working copies within 30 days unless the client renews or law requires retention
- Retain minimum suppression data needed to prevent prohibited re-contact

## Required Documents

Prepare before accepting client data:

1. One-page sprint scope
2. Service agreement
3. Confidentiality/data terms
4. Client outreach authorization
5. Eligible-record attestation
6. Script and cadence approval
7. Refund/cancellation policy
8. Internal do-not-call procedure
9. Data-retention checklist
10. Final report template

### Agreement Topics

- Parties and independent-contractor status
- Exact scope and 20-record cap
- Client responsibilities and data accuracy
- Representation that records involve legitimate prior commercial proposals
- Authorization to contact specified businesses
- No guarantee of replies, meetings, awards, revenue, or ROI
- No authority for JanSan Sweep to bind the client or modify pricing
- Payment, cancellation, and refunds
- Confidentiality and safeguards
- Prohibited records and campaigns
- Opt-out handling
- Termination rights
- Data deletion/return
- Liability, indemnity, governing law, and dispute terms reviewed by counsel

See `02-OFFER-AND-PRICING.md` for the refund policy draft that should be incorporated into the agreement.
