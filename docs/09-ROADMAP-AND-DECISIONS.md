# JanSan Sweep — Roadmap, Risk, and Decision Log

## 30-Day Launch Plan

### Day 1

- Upload the full knowledge base to Claude Projects
- Paste the Project Instructions
- Create the prospect tracker
- Create the 20-record import template
- Draft scope, agreement, and authorization documents
- Select the payment method
- Create the email signature
- Role-play 10 calls with Claude

### Day 2

- Reverify 25 high-priority prospects
- Make at least 40 calls
- Record exact objections
- Send only relevant personalized follow-up
- Book Bid Audits

### Day 3

- Call another 25 firms
- Reattempt high-priority owners at different times
- Hold audits
- Ask directly for prepaid pilots
- Revise only language repeatedly causing confusion

### Days 4–5

- Continue until at least 10 qualified owner conversations occur
- Close the first pilot
- Complete intake and eligibility review
- Keep selling while preparing delivery

### Days 6–12

- Deliver the first sprint
- Track time and outcomes
- Hand off interest immediately
- Continue 20–30 JanSan Sweep call attempts daily
- Close a second pilot if capacity permits

### Days 13–14

- Conduct the client closeout
- Request an honest assessment, verified case-study permission, and referral
- Review economics
- Decide whether to retain $199 or test $299

### Days 15–21

- Publish the one-page website
- Include verified proof only with permission
- Target another 75 firms
- Test one alternative opener while keeping the offer constant

### Days 22–30

- Complete up to three pilots
- Compare acquisition, qualification, close, response, and time data
- Offer a retainer only to clients with recurring proposal volume
- Decide whether to continue, refine, change scope/price, or stop
- Build no software unless all build triggers are met

## Daily Founder Routine

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 30 minutes | Verify and prioritize prospects |
| 90 minutes | First owner call block |
| 30 minutes | Send requested material and book audits |
| 60–120 minutes | Deliver current client work |
| 30 minutes | Second call block in another time zone |
| 15 minutes | Update metrics and set tomorrow's first task |

Keep build and administrative tasks outside the strongest calling windows.

## Risk Register

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Client supplies cold/consumer contacts | High | Attestation, sampling, and rejection rights |
| Misleading identity | High | Identify JanSan Sweep and the client relationship |
| Automated-call exposure | High | Manual calls only initially |
| State-law variation | High | Narrow launch and legal review before expansion |
| Guaranteed-results expectation | Medium | Explicit scope and disclaimers |
| Poor client data | Medium | Required template and preflight audit |
| Client fails to handle interest | High | Named handoff contact and response expectation |
| Price too low | Medium | Track time, cap scope, and test higher pricing |
| Data breach | High | Minimize data, use 2FA, restrict access, delete files |
| Founder overbuilds | High | Enforce revenue-before-software rule |
| Weak retention | Medium | Measure actual outcomes and ongoing proposal volume |
| Attribution dispute | Medium | Avoid pure commission and define outcomes |

## Software Build Triggers

Build an internal tool only when:

- Three paid pilots have been delivered
- The same bottleneck appears in all three
- Time saved can be estimated
- Workflow fields are stable
- Development will not stop active selling

The likely first tool is a private operations console that imports CSV files, validates fields, assigns dispositions, schedules manual tasks, records opt-outs, and creates client reports. Version one should not automatically call, text, or email.

## First-Hire Triggers

Do not hire before recurring revenue supports it. The first support person should be experienced in B2B calling, comfortable with owners and facility managers, calm and non-pushy, strong at notes and CRM discipline, able to follow identity and opt-out rules, and paid for time and quality — not solely for appointments.
