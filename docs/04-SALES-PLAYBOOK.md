# JanSan Sweep — Sales Playbook

## Cold-Call Scripts

### Gatekeeper

> Hi, this is Clay with JanSan Sweep. I'm trying to reach the owner or whoever handles commercial cleaning proposals. It's about older proposals that never received a clear decision—not supplies or advertising. Who would that be?

### Owner Opener

> Hi, [Name], this is Clay with JanSan Sweep. I'll be brief. I help owner-led commercial cleaning companies follow up on walkthroughs and proposals that went quiet. I'm not selling leads. Do you have commercial quotes from the last six to twelve months that never gave you a firm yes, no, or rebid date?

Stop and listen.

### If Yes

> That's exactly the inventory I work. I run a seven-business-day JanSan Sweep Sprint. You send up to 20 legitimate old proposals, approve the contact language, and I personally follow up. You get a dashboard showing who is still open, who wants a revision, who has a future rebid date, and which records are dead. I'm offering the first three pilots for $199 prepaid. About how many unresolved commercial proposals do you think you have?

### If Unsure

> Where do completed proposals live today—email, estimating software, spreadsheets, or a CRM? If we looked back 90 days, could we identify ten where a business requested a quote or walkthrough but no final decision was recorded?

### If They Already Follow Up

> That's good. How many touches does each proposal reliably receive, and do you record the loss reason or future rebid date? If every proposal is consistently worked and closed, you probably don't need JanSan Sweep.

### Close

> Based on what you described, the pilot fits. It is $199 prepaid for up to 20 qualified proposals over seven business days. The next step is a 15-minute intake, the service agreement, payment, and your export. Is [time A] or [time B] better?

### Voicemail

> Hi, [Name], this is Clay with JanSan Sweep at [phone]. I help commercial cleaning owners get clear outcomes from older walkthroughs and proposals that went quiet. This is not a lead-selling call. Again, Clay with JanSan Sweep at [phone].

## Discovery Call

### Fifteen-Minute Agenda

1. Confirm recurring commercial cleaning
2. Quantify proposal activity and backlog
3. Understand current follow-up ownership
4. Test record quality and authorization
5. Confirm capacity to handle interest
6. Explain scope, limits, and deliverables
7. Ask for prepaid payment and schedule intake

### Discovery Questions

- Walk me through what happens after you send a proposal.
- Who owns the second, third, and fourth follow-up?
- How many proposals did you send during the last 90 days?
- How many have a final recorded outcome?
- What is a typical monthly account worth?
- Which old opportunities would you most like clarified?
- What information do you retain with each proposal?
- If someone asks for a revision, how quickly can you respond?
- Are any records consumers, purchased contacts, government bids, or opt-outs?
- Can you service another recurring account right now?

### Qualification Standard

Sell the sprint when the client has at least 10 legitimate records, meaningful recurring commercial services, sufficient business-contact context, capacity to act on interest, a named handoff person, and willingness to approve scripts and pay upfront.

## Objection Handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| I can do this myself. | You absolutely can. The question is whether it is consistently getting done while you manage crews, clients, and new estimates. |
| Those leads are dead. | Some will be. The useful outcomes are delayed decisions, changed contacts, revision requests, and future rebid dates. Closing dead records is also valuable. |
| Can you guarantee a contract? | No. Nobody can honestly guarantee the prospect's decision. JanSan Sweep guarantees the agreed work and reporting, not an award. |
| Pay only if you close one. | Closing depends on your pricing, capacity, revisions, and the buyer's process. The pilot pays for defined work. We can discuss hybrid terms after real baseline data exists. |
| We use a CRM. | A CRM is useful if every proposal is updated and worked. Are any legitimate proposals still sitting without a defined next action? |
| I don't want prospects bothered. | Neither do I. You approve every record, the wording, cadence, caller identity, and stop rules. Opt-outs are honored immediately. |
| Why only $199? | It is a founding pilot limited to 20 records so the workflow and outcomes can be measured with the first three clients. |
| Send information. | Happy to. Before I send something generic, do you have at least ten unresolved commercial proposals? If yes, I'll send the one-page scope. |
| We don't have a list. | If the proposals exist in email or estimating software, we can discuss a small audit. The sprint cannot begin until qualified records are identified. |

## Role-Play Scoring Criteria

When practicing with Claude, score each call on:

1. Opener clarity — was the purpose stated in one breath without jargon?
2. Relevance — did it immediately connect to a problem the owner recognizes?
3. Discovery — did questions uncover real inventory and follow-up gaps?
4. Credibility — did the pitch avoid overpromising or vague claims?
5. Risk reversal — was the low-cost, limited-scope pilot framed clearly?
6. Objection handling — were objections addressed directly, not dodged?
7. Ask — did the call end with a specific, direct request for payment or next step?
8. Outcome — was a concrete next step secured (time, payment, or clear no)?
