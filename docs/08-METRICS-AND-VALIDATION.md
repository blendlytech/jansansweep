# JanSan Sweep — Metrics and Validation

## JanSan Sweep Sales Metrics

- Prospects researched
- Manual attempts
- Owner conversations
- Qualified conversations
- Bid Audits booked
- Bid Audits held
- Pilots offered
- Pilots paid
- Cash collected
- Reasons lost
- Days from first contact to payment

## Delivery Metrics

- Records submitted
- Records accepted
- Rejection reasons
- Call attempts
- Emails sent
- Substantive conversations/replies
- Recovered-status records
- Review requests
- Revision requests
- Rebid dates
- Clear losses
- Bad data
- Do-not-contact requests
- No-response records
- Founder time by task
- Handoff speed

## Core Formulas

- Contact rate = owner conversations / call attempts
- Qualification rate = qualified conversations / owner conversations
- Audit show rate = audits held / audits booked
- Close rate = pilots paid / qualified calls or held audits
- Accepted-record rate = accepted / submitted records
- Substantive-response rate = substantive responses / accepted records
- Recovered-status rate = useful new outcomes / accepted records
- Effective delivery rate = collected revenue / delivery hours

Do not use "revenue recovered" as a metric unless the client's collected revenue and attribution are documented.

## Validation Experiments

### Problem Test

**Hypothesis:** At least 5 of 25 owner conversations reveal 10 or more unresolved commercial proposals.
**Test:** Speak with 25 owner/operators.
**Pass:** Five confirm sufficient inventory and inconsistent follow-up.
**Failure response:** Determine whether the wrong segment, wording, or proposal-volume threshold caused the result.

### Paid Offer Test

**Hypothesis:** At least one qualified owner will prepay $199.
**Test:** Make a direct offer to 10 qualified owners.
**Pass:** One pays.
**Failure response:** Analyze trust, scope, proof, risk, urgency, and payment — not just price.

### Inventory Test

**Hypothesis:** At least 70% of submitted records satisfy eligibility and data requirements.
**Test:** Audit the first three client files.
**Pass:** At least 14 of 20 records are usable on average.
**Failure response:** Tighten pre-sale qualification and provide an export template.

### Delivery Test

**Hypothesis:** A 20-record sprint can be delivered in seven hours or fewer.
**Test:** Track all work.
**Pass:** Seven hours or fewer without reducing quality or compliance.
**Failure response:** Reduce scope, increase price, require cleaner inputs, or automate only the repeated administrative bottleneck.

### Retainer Test

**Hypothesis:** At least one of the first three clients wants ongoing follow-up.
**Test:** Conduct closeout interviews and present a monthly option.
**Pass:** One paid retainer or strong written buying intent at a viable price.
**Failure response:** Keep the business sprint-based until repeat demand exists.

These thresholds are founder-set decision rules, not industry benchmarks.

## Stop or Pivot Rules

Pause and diagnose when:

- After 200 targeted attempts, fewer than 15 owner conversations occur: list/contact problem
- After 25 owner conversations, fewer than five have sufficient inventory: ICP/problem issue
- After 10 qualified offers, nobody pays $199: trust, offer, urgency, or value issue
- Most submitted records lack legitimate prior engagement: eligibility issue
- Delivery repeatedly exceeds 10 hours: economic issue
- Buyers strongly resist transparent third-party follow-up: delivery-model issue
- Counsel finds unacceptable risk: compliance-model issue
- Clients fail to respond promptly to reopened interest: customer-readiness issue

Do not pivot merely because several calls are uncomfortable or one prospect objects.
